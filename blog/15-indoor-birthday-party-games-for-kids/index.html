15 indoor birthday party games for kids

Parties 7 min read 17 November 2026

An indoor birthday party can feel daunting: a handful of excited children, one living room, and a long afternoon ahead. The good news is that you do not need a big space or a big budget. You need a plan, a rhythm, and a few games that work in a small room. Here are fifteen of our favourites, all made with things most homes already have.

Before you start: a few simple rules

Indoor parties go best when the space is ready before the first guest rings the bell. A little preparation saves a lot of stress later.

  • Clear one area of breakables and move sharp-cornered furniture aside.
  • Decide which rooms are "party rooms" and close the others.
  • Keep a calm corner with books or colouring for children who need a break.
  • Plan games in short blocks of 10 to 15 minutes, alternating busy games with quiet ones.
  • Have the prizes or treats in a box you can reach, not on display.

A word on safety: balloons, small beads, coins and tiny prizes are choking hazards for young children. If any guests are under three, or if younger siblings are around, keep these items out of reach and supervise any game that uses them. Burst balloon pieces should be picked up straight away.

Busy games to burn off energy

1. Freeze dance

Play music and let everyone dance. When the music stops, everyone freezes like a statue. Instead of eliminating children who wobble, give them a silly job, like being the next "music stopper". Nobody sits out, and everyone stays in the game.

2. Balloon keep-up

Blow up one or two balloons and challenge the group to keep them off the floor. Add rules as they get better: only elbows, only heads, or count the taps out loud together. Keep a grown-up nearby and clear away any burst balloon straight away.

3. Animal walk relay

Make two teams and a start and finish line with tape or cushions. Each child crosses the room moving like an animal you call out: a crab, a frog, a penguin or a bear. It is noisy, funny and works in a narrow hallway.

4. Musical cushions

A gentler version of musical chairs. Scatter cushions on the floor, one per child. When the music stops, everyone finds a cushion. Remove one cushion each round, but instead of anyone leaving, children share cushions until the whole group is squeezed onto just a few.

5. Simon says

A classic for a reason. It needs nothing at all and works for almost any age. Let the birthday child be Simon for a round; they will love being in charge.

Calmer games for between the busy ones

6. What is in the sock?

Put small household objects into old socks: a spoon, a toy car, a sponge, a pine cone. Children feel the sock without looking and guess what is inside. Use larger objects if little ones are playing.

7. Kim’s game

Lay out eight to ten objects on a tray. Everyone looks for thirty seconds, then you cover the tray and secretly remove one item. Who can say what is missing? It is a lovely memory game and a good way to settle a group before food.

8. Pass the parcel

Wrap a small prize in several layers of paper, with a sticker or a riddle between the layers. Pass the parcel while the music plays; whoever holds it when the music stops unwraps one layer. If you put something in every layer, every child gets a turn to win something.

9. Draw the cake

Give each child a paper plate and crayons and ask them to draw their dream birthday cake. Then hold a little exhibition where every drawing wins a category: the tallest, the most colourful, the silliest, the most candles.

10. Charades for small actors

Write or draw simple actions on cards: brushing your teeth, flying like a bird, eating spaghetti, opening a present. Children act them out without words while the others guess. For non-readers, picture cards work perfectly.

Games with a story

Children love a party with a "mission". A simple story turns separate games into one adventure, and it gives you a natural way to move from one activity to the next.

11. Treasure hunt with clues

Hide a series of clues around the house, each one leading to the next, with a small treasure at the end. For children who cannot read yet, use picture clues: a drawing of the bathtub, the fridge, the sofa. Our post on running an escape room at home has more ideas for clues that work for young players.

12. Detective mission

Tell the group that the birthday cake (or the party hat, or the teddy) has gone missing. Hide it, then give children simple clues to work out where it is. Every guest is a detective, and the mystery ends with the cake being found just in time.

13. Code breaker

Create a simple code where each picture or colour stands for a letter. Children decode a short secret word, like "CAKE" or the birthday child’s name. Older guests can work in pairs and help younger ones.

14. Scavenger hunt bingo

Give each child or team a card with pictures of things to find indoors: something red, something soft, a spoon, a book with an animal on the cover. They tick off each one as they find it.

15. The big finale

End with a moment that brings everyone together: opening a "treasure box" of party bags, a group song, or a parade around the living room. A clear ending helps children wind down before parents arrive.

Putting it all together

Fifteen games is more than enough for one party. For a 90-minute celebration with children aged 5 to 7, a good rhythm might be:

  1. Welcome activity as guests arrive (drawing or a crown to colour)
  2. Two busy games
  3. One calm game
  4. Food and cake
  5. A story-based game, like the treasure hunt
  6. The finale

For three- and four-year-olds, keep everything shorter and simpler, and plan for more grown-up help. For older children, add more challenge, team play and code-breaking.

A final tip

Children rarely remember whether the decorations matched. They remember laughing with their friends, the moment they found the treasure, and the feeling of being celebrated. Keep it simple, keep it moving, and leave room for a little silliness. Happy birthday to your little one.
